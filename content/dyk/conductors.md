# Willi Boskovsky (1955-1979)

Kids: Did you know? For 25 years, the person leading the New Year’s Concert didn’t just wave a baton, he actually conducted with his violin\! **Willi Boskovsky** played and led at the very same time, exactly the way Johann Strauss did long ago. Can you find the violin in his photo? \[insert AI photo of Boskovsky with violin in hand here\]![][image1]

Adults: Did you know? **Willi Boskovsky** led the New Year’s Concert a record 25 times, between 1955 and 1979\. That’s more often than any other conductor in the orchestra’s history. A longtime concertmaster of the Vienna Philharmonic, he was handed the podium after the unexpected death of Clemens Krauss in 1954, and he led in the old Viennese manner, regularly directing the orchestra from the violin just as Johann Strauss I had done. 

Experts / nerds:  Did you know? **Boskovsky**’s quarter-century leading the *Neujahrskonzert* revived the 19th-century ‘Vorgeiger’ tradition — directing while playing the solo violin, in emulation of Johann Strauss. His cycle of recordings with Decca effectively fixed the post-war sound of the concert for a global television audience. His run ended only when he suffered a stroke in January 1979, which forced him to step down. The orchestra's response — initially engaging Lorin Maazel through 1986, then switching to a different conductor every year — is the reason the podium rotates to this day.

# Herbert von Karajan (1987)

**Kids:** Did you know? The famous conductor **Herbert von Karajan** led the New Year's Concert just *once*, in 1987 — and he did something nobody had ever done before: he invited a *singer\!* Soprano Kathleen Battle sang a sparkling waltz called ‘Voices of Spring’, or in German the *Frühlingsstimmenwaltzer*.

> ![][image2]

**Adults:** Did you know? **Herbert von Karajan** conducted the New Year's Concert only once, on 1 January 1987 — and it opened with the Overture to *Die Fledermaus*. His key innovation was to bring in a vocal soloist for the first time: Karajan invited Kathleen Battle, a star at the Metropolitan Opera in New York at the time, to sing Johann Strauss' *Frühlingsstimmenwalzer*… the first time a singer had been engaged in the concert series. The live Deutsche Grammophon recording was made at the Musikverein in January 1987 and became one of the best-loved in the series. 

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? **Karajan**’s sole New Year’s Concert came near the end of his life (he died in 1989), and it was the first ever to feature a vocal soloist. The gesture was a deliberate historical echo: at Clemens Krauss’s very first Strauss-family concert in Salzburg in 1929, soprano Adele Kern had sung Voices of Spring, and Karajan now repeated the idea with Kathleen Battle. Fittingly for this exhibit, the programme opened with the *Fledermaus* Overture — the same 1987 reading in our corpus here — a notably relaxed version compared to quicker Vienna Philharmonic renditions in the years around it

# Clemens Krauss (1939, 1941–45, 1948–54)

**Kids:** Did you know? The New Year’s Concert had to be invented by *somebody*, and that somebody was a conductor named **Clemens Krauss.** He started the whole tradition way back in 1939, and the very first year it happened on New Year’s Eve, not on New Year’s Day\!

**Adults:** Did you know? **Clemens Krauss** founded the New Year’s Concert and led it until his death in 1954, except for two years. The very first one took place on 31 December 1939 — the only edition ever held on New Year's Eve rather than New Year’s Day (it moved to 1 January in 1941). That first programme featured only music by Johann Strauss II and ended with the Overture to *Die Fledermaus* — one of the pieces at the heart of this museum exhibit.

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? **Krauss** (1893–1954) launched the concert during the darkest chapter in the orchestra's history: the 1939 ‘*außerordentliches Konzert*’ \[non-subscription concert\] was devised with the support of Vienna’s Nazi *Gauleiter* Baldur von Schirach and dedicated to the *Kriegswinterhilfswerk* \[Wintertime War Relief\] organization. It was quickly taken up for propaganda purposes, a history the Vienna Philharmonic itself now openly acknowledges. Krauss conducted the concert in 1939, 1941–45 and 1948–54; only the second half of the 1954 concert was ever recorded live, so his famous Decca discs were actually taped in the studio each summer as a memento for the growing radio audience — which is the origin of the 1951–54 set of recordings you can listen to in this museum exhibit\!

# Carlos Kleiber (1989, 1992\)

**Kids:** Did you know? One of the most famous conductors in the world almost *never* performed in public — but **Carlos Kleiber** loved this Viennese music so much that he led the New Year's Concert twice, in 1989 and 1992\. People were so excited that those concerts sold out in hours just from a *rumour* that he might appear\!

**Adults:** Did you know? **Carlos Kleiber** — son of the great conductor Erich Kleiber, and himself often voted the greatest conductor of the 20th century — was famously reclusive and conducted remarkably rarely. He made an exception for this repertoire, leading the New Year’s Concert in 1989 and 1992 (his 1992 appearance fell in the orchestra’s 150th-anniversary year). Both are preserved on video, and his 1989 *Fledermaus Overture* is widely considered one of the greatest performances of the piece ever recorded.

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? **Kleiber**’s two New Year’s Concerts (1989, 1992\) sit within one of the most sparing careers in conducting history — Kleiber reportedly turned down the Berlin Philharmonic yet said yes to Vienna, drawn to the Viennese idiom. *Die Fledermaus* was a lifelong preoccupation of Kleibers (he is said to have conducted the operetta some 78 times), and the 1989 overture is frequently cited as his definitive account. A measure of his eccentricity: for one benefit concert he accepted, in lieu of a fee, a new Audi built to his particular specifications.

# Josef Krips (1946, 1947\)

**Kids:** Did you know? After the Second World War ended, someone had to keep the young New Year's Concerts going, and that person was **Josef Krips.** He led the series in 1946 and 1947, and 1946 was the very first time the concert included the Radetzky March… the march everyone claps along to at the  end\!

**Adults:** Did you know? **Josef Krips** (1902–1974) conducted the New Year's Concert in 1946 and 1947, stepping in while the concert’s founder, Clemens Krauss, served a two-year conducting ban imposed by the Allies. Krips, whose father was Jewish, and who had been barred from working under the Nazi regime, was one of the very few conductors cleared to perform in post-war Austria, and he became central to rebuilding Vienna’s musical life, reopening both the Vienna State Opera and, in 1946, the Salzburg Festival. The Radetzky March was heard at the New Year's Concert for the first time during his 1946 performance.

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? Krips’s two concerts sit precisely in the gap left by Krauss’ denazification ban (1946–47). After the 1938 *Anschlus*s Krips, whose father was Jewish, had lost his Vienna State Opera post and his professorship, and his musical activity was effectively suspended through the war. This is why he could come in with ‘clean hands,’ and prove indispensable to the reconstruction, leading the first post-war Vienna Philharmonic tours, the reopening of the State Opera, and the 1946 Salzburg Festival conducting *Don Giovanni.* The Radetzky March made its concert debut under him in 1946 (although it and the Blue Danube waltz only became the fixed twin encores beginning in 1958). Krips went on to lead the London Symphony Orchestra, the San Francisco Symphony and the Metropolitan Opera.

# Gustavo Dudamel (2017)

**Kids:** Did you know? The youngest person ever to conduct the New Year’s Concert came all the way from Venezuela\! **Gustavo Dudamel** led it in 2017\. He learned music as a child in a programme called *El Sistema* that donates instruments to kids in poorer areas of the world — proof you can start small and end up on the world’s most famous stage\!

**Adults:** Did you know? When Venezuelan conductor **Gustavo Dudamel** made his New Year's Concert debut in 2017, he became the youngest conductor in the event’s history. A product of Venezuela’s *El Sistema* music-education programme, he first performed with the Vienna Philharmonic in 2007; his 2017 concert also marked the orchestra’s 175th anniversary. Dudamel is now Music & Artistic Director of the Los Angeles Philharmonic and, from 2026, of the New York Philharmonic.

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? **Dudamel**’s 2017 concert (and at age 35 as the youngest conductor featured in the series’ history) is emblematic of the event’s current, global reach. Broadcast to more than 90 countries and some 50 million viewers according to the orchestra’s data, his appearance coincided with the 175th-anniversary year of the Vienna Philharmonic. An *El Sistema* alumnus with a genuinely mainstream reach (Dudamel has featured at the Super Bowl, on Hollywood score recordings, and countless festival stages) he embodies the concert’s modern role as a worldwide cultural phenomenon rather than a purely Viennese event.

# Lorin Maazel (1980-1986, 1994, 1996, 1999, 2005\)

**Kids:** Did you know? Before Lorin Maazel, every conductor of the New Year’s Concert had been Austrian. In 1980 he became the very first from another country. And here’s the fun part — he wasn’t only a conductor. He was also a brilliant violinist, and sometimes during the concerts he would put down the baton, pick up a violin, and play along too\!

**Adults:** Did you know? In 1980, the American conductor Lorin Maazel became the first non-Austrian conductor of the New Year’s concert, ending four decades in which only Austrians had been at the podium. A child prodigy who conducted major American orchestras before he was even a teenager, Maazel was also an accomplished violinist, and appeared as a violin soloist during five of his own New Year’s Concerts. All told, he conducted the concert eleven times between 1980 and 2005, among the most of any conductor in the series’ history.

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? Maazel’s 1980 debut broke the concert’s unbroken Austrian succession (Krauss, Krips, Boskovsky) and opened its international era, just before the orchestra abandoned the single-conductor model for the series in favor of annual guests. A second-generation American born near Paris, Maazel began violin lessons at age five and conducting at seven, and conducted many prominent American orchestras between the ages of nine and fifteen. That he repeatedly doubled as violin soloist at the concert carried on the tradition of the violinist-conductors, which included Strauss Jr. himself, as well as Boskovsky. Maazel’s eleven concerts also bracket two of the exhibit’s notable years, including the 1982 stage protest, the 2005 concert that dropped the Radetzky March for tsunami victims, and played the whip during the  Jockey-Polka in 1996\.

# Nikolaus Harnoncourt (2001, 2003\)

**Kids:** Did you know? Nikolaus Harnoncourt just loved finding out how music *used* to sound, long ago. When he conducted the New Year’s Concert, he liked to bring back old-fashioned sounds that other conductors had quietly dropped — including a real train horn in Strauss’ *Vergnügungszug* (Pleasure Train) polka\!

**Adults:** Did you know? Nikolaus Harnoncourt came to the New Year’s Concert — which he conducted in 2001 and 2003 — from a very different world. Starting out as a cellist, he founded the period-instrument ensemble *Concentus Musicus Wien* in 1953 and became a pioneer of the early-music movement, devoted to asking how exactly a composer’s own audience would have heard the music. He had been a cellist in the Vienna Symphony from 1952 to 1969 before turning to conducting, and he brought that same curiosity to Strauss, treating these familiar waltzes and polkas as music to be freshly re-examined. 

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know?  Harnoncourt’s two concerts imported the scrutiny of the early-music movement into the most tradition-bound event in the repertoire. A cellist in the Vienna Symphony from 1952 to 1969, he left to conduct and to build Concentus Musicus Wien into a flagship of historically informed  period performance; his later “Walzer Revolution” project applied the same source-driven thinking to Mozart, Strauss and Lanner. 

# Georges Prêtre (2008, 2010\)

**Kids:** Did you know? Most conductors of the New Year’s Concert were middle-aged, and a few quite young when they first led it. Georges Prêtre didn’t conduct it until he was *83 years old\!* He was also the very first conductor from France ever invited.

**Adults:** Did you know? When Georges Prêtre led the New Year’s Concert in 2008, he became the first (and so far, the only) French conductor appointed. He didn’t make his debut with the series until the ripe old age of 83\. A celebrated opera conductor and the preferred maestro of soprano Maria Callas, Prêtre returned for a second concert in 2010 and then kept conducting well into his nineties.

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? Prêtre (1924–2017) remains both the most senior conductor and the sole Frenchman invited to lead the New Year’s Concert. He made his series debut at 83, in 2008, and returned in 2010\. He gave the 2008 programme a French accent, opening it with Johann Strauss II’s Napoleon March, op. 156  in its first New Year’s Concert performance. Long associated with the Vienna Symphony, the Philharmonic’s sister orchestra, where Prêtre was principal conductor from 1986 to 1991, he embodied the guest-conductor era’s reach beyond the German-speaking world. 

# Zubin Mehta (1990, 1995, 1998, 2007, 2015\)

**Kids:** Did you know? Zubin Mehta grew up in India, thousands of miles from Vienna — but as a young man he moved to Vienna to learn to conduct, and went on to lead the New Year’s Concert five times. His very first music teacher was his own dad, who had started an orchestra back home in Mumbai.

**Adults:** Did you know? The Indian conductor Zubin Mehta, born in Mumbai in 1936, came to Vienna as a teenager — moving there in 1954 to study conducting under Hans Swarowsky at the state music academy, the institution that is today the mdw-University of Music and Performing Arts Vienna, the university who created this museum exhibit\! Mehta went on to conduct the New Year’s Concert five times — in 1990, 1995, 1998, 2007 and 2015 — as part of his lifelong affiliation with the Vienna Philharmonic.

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? Mehta’s conducting teacher during his studies in Vienna was Hans Swarowsky, who also taught two other future New Year’s Concert conductors: Claudio Abbado and Maris Jansons. One of the two most popular guest-conductors of the modern era of the series — alongside Riccardo Muti — Only Willi Boskovsky, Clemens Krauss, Lorin Maazel, and Riccardo Muti conducted the series more often than Mehta.

# Riccardo Muti (1993, 1997, 2000, 2004, 2018, 2021, 2025\)

**Kids:** Did you know? Some conductors lead the New Year’s concert once or twice, but Italian conductor Riccardo muti has done it seven times — more than almost anyone in modern times\! He even conducted it in 2021, the strange year during the covid pandemic where the hall was almost empty.

**Adults:** Did you know? The Italian conductor Riccardo Muti, born in Naples in 1941, has led the New Year’s Concert seven times — in 1993, 1997, 2000, 2004, 2018, 2021 and 2025 — making him, with Zubin Mehta, one of the two most engaged New Year’s Concert conductors since the era of Lorin Maazel. His bond with the Vienna Philharmonic, which he first conducted in 1971, runs deep, and it fell to Muti to conduct the extraordinary 2021 concert, played to an empty hall during the pandemic.

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? Muti’s seven New Year’s Concerts span more than three decades and bracket two milestone moments: the pandemic concert of 2021, played to an empty Musikverein, and the 2025 concert marking the 200th anniversary of Johann Strauss II’s birth. An honorary member of the Vienna Philharmonic since 2011, Muti is also known, apart from Vienna, for his “Roads of Friendship” concerts in places marked by conflict — Sarajevo, Beirut, Jerusalem, Damascus — a reminder that even conductor’s podiums sometimes use music as soft diplomacy. 

# Claudio Abbado (1988, 1991\)

**Kids:** Did you know? When Claudio Abbado was young, his friend Zubin Mehta told him he should go and study conducting in Vienna — so he did\! Abbado grew up to lead the New Year’s Concert twice, and he loved starting brand-new orchestras just for young musicians. 

**Adults:** Did you know? The great Italian conductor Claudio Abbado led the New Year’s Concert in 1988 and 1991, during his years as music director of the Vienna State Opera. Like Mehta and Jansons, he had trained in Vienna under conductor Hans Swarowsky — in fact he studied with Swarowsky on Zubin Mehta’s personal recommendation\! After Karajan’s death in 1989, the Berlin Philharmonic chose Abbado as his successor, and throughout his life he poured energy into founding orchestras for young players. 

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? Abbado completes the strong lineage to conductor Hanss Swarowsky that runs through the New Year’s Concert. He, Mehta and Jansons all studied with the Viennese pedagogue. Abbado’s two concerts fell within his 1986–1991 tenure as music director of the Vienna State Opera. A tireless founder of youth ensembles — the European (Union) Youth Orchestra and the Gustav Mahler Jugendorchester among them — he carried that forward-looking spirit from Vienna to Berlin when he succeeded Karajan at the Philharmonic.

# Seiji Ozawa (2002)

**Kids:** Did you know? As a boy, Seiji Ozawa dreamed of being a concert pianist — until he hurt his fingers playing rugby\! So he became a conductor instead, and in 2002 was the very first conductor from Japan ever to lead the New Year’s Concert.

**Adults:** Did you know? In 2002, Seiji Ozawa became the first Japanese conductor to lead the New Year’s Concert, and the live recording broke sales records in Japan. Born in Japanese-occupied Manchuria in 1935, Ozawa remains the only conductor ever to have studied under both Herbert von Karajan and Leonard Bernstein. He also led the Boston Symphony for 29 years and was its longest-serving music director. Ozawa took up the Vienna State Opera directorship the very same year as his New Year’s Concert.

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? Ozawa’s single New Year’s Concert (2002) crowned a pivotal year for the conductor. Having just closed a record 29-year tenure in Boston, he was named principal conductor of the Vienna State Opera, a post he held until 2010\. Mentored by Munch, Karajan and Bernstein, he was the first Japanese conductor to achieve this degree of success in the West, and also founded Japan’s Saito Kinen Orchestra and conducted a globe-spanning “Ode to Joy” at the 1998 Nagano Olympics. 

# Mariss Jansons (2006, 2012, 2016\)

**Kids:** Did you know? Have you listened to the train polka where the conductor honks a funny, out-of-tune horn? That was Mariss Jansons, in 2016\! He led the New Year’s Concert three times and loved making the music feel wonderfully alive. His father was a famous conductor too. 

**Adults:** Did you know? Mariss Jansons, who conducted the New Year’s Concert in 2006, 2012 and 2016, had one of the most dramatic life stories of any conductor. Born in Riga in 1943 while his mother, a Jewish singer, was in hiding during the war, he studied in Leningrad and then — like Zubin Mehta — in Vienna under Hans Swarowsky. In his 2016 concert he leaned joyfully into Strauss’s train effects in Vergnügungszug, even taking up a horn himself — you can find it and listen yourself by clicking on it in this exhibit\!

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? Jansons’s three concerts carried an unusually personal weight. His father, the conductor Arvīds Jansons, collapsed and died while conducting in 1984; twelve years later, in 1996 Mariss himself nearly died of a heart attack while conducting the final pages of La bohème in Oslo, and lived his remaining decades with an implanted defibrillator. That he brought such evident delight to the New Year’s Concerts is particularly amazing considering that dark history… Like his fellow New Year’s Concert series conductor, Zubin Mehta, Jansons was a pupil in Vienna of conductor Hans Swarowsky.

# Daniel Barenboim (2009, 2014, 2022\)

**Kids:** Did you know? Daniel Barenboim was so good at the piano that he gave his very first concert at just *seven* years old\! He grew up to be both a famous pianist *and* a conductor, led the New Year’s Concert three times, and started a special orchestra that brings together young musicians from countries that don’t always get along. 

**Adults:** Did you know? Daniel Barenboim — the Argentine-Israeli musician who conducted the New Year’s Concert in 2009, 2014 and 2022 — is that rare figure equally celebrated as pianist and conductor, having made his debut as a pianist in 1950, aged only seven. With the Palestinian-American scholar Edward Said, he founded the West-Eastern Divan Orchestra in 1999, an ensemble of young musicians from across the Middle East, Israelis and Arabs playing side by side. At his 2009 New Year’s Concert he used his address to the audience specifically to voice hope for peace.

**Experts / nerds:** Barenboim first appeared with the Vienna Philharmonic, as a pianist, in 1965\. The conductor, who holds Argentine, Israeli, Spanish and Palestinian citizenships, has led the Berlin State Opera and its Staatskappelle for three decades, from 1992 until 2023, and was a founding member of the West-Eastern Divan Orchestra, which he launched with Palestinian-American scholar Edward Said in 1999\. You can hear him in this exhibition conducting one of the most recent renditions of *Die Fledermaus* overture; his is one of the few renditions which adopts the revised critical-edition note in its opening, changing what had long been played as an ‘E’ in bar 7 to a ‘D’...

# Franz Welser-Möst (2011, 2013, 2023\)

**Kids:** Did you know? Franz Welser-Möst comes from Austria — the home of the Strauss family and of the New Year’s Concert itself. He has led the concert three times, and when he’s not in Vienna he leads a famous orchestra far across the ocean, in Cleveland, America 

**Adults:** Did you know? The Austrian conductor Franz Welser-Möst, born in Linz in 1960, has led the New Year’s Concert three times — in 2011, 2013 and 2023\. He has been music director of the Cleveland Orchestra since 2002, its longest-serving music director, and in 2010 he succeeded Seiji Ozawa as artistic director of the Vienna State Opera, a post he held until 2014\. 

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? Welser-Möst’s Vienna ties reach back decades: his very first appearance at the Vienna State Opera, in 1987, was as a substitute for Claudio Abbado in Rossini’s *L’italiana in Algeri,* and he later ran the house himself as general music director (2010–2014), directly succeeding Seiji Ozawa — both conductors who appear elsewhere in this exhibit. He is an honorary member of the Vienna Philharmonic and holds its Honorary Ring, and his three New Year’s Concerts run in parallel with his record-length Cleveland tenure, where he has been at the helm since 2002\. 

# Christian Thielemann (2019, 2024\)

**Kids:** Did you know? Christian Thielemann grew up in Berlin and got his big break as a young assistant to one of the most famous conductors in the world, Herbert von Karajan. He has led the New Year’s Concert twice. 

**Adults:** Did you know? The German conductor Christian Thielemann, born in Berlin in 1959, conducted the New Year’s Concert in 2019 and 2024\. He launched his career as a répétiteur (rehearsal pianist/conductor) at the Deutsche Oper Berlin in 1978, and the following year Herbert von Karajan engaged him as his assistant. He has recorded the complete Beethoven and Bruckner symphonies with the Vienna Philharmonic and is widely regarded as a leading interpreter of the German Romantic repertoire,  particularly Wagner, Bruckner and (Richard) Strauss.

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? Thielemann’s career connects to two conductors elsewhere in this exhibit: Karajan took him on as an assistant in 1979 (after which he went on to assist Daniel Barenboim at the Bayreuth Festival), and from the 2024/25 season he became general music director of the Berlin State Opera and the Staatskapelle Berlin — the post Barenboim had held for three decades. Thielemann was also principal conductor of the Staatskapelle Dresden from 2012 to 2024 and is a mainstay of the Bayreuth Festival. 

# Andris Nelsons (2020)

**Kids:** Did you know? Before he was a conductor, Andris Nelsons was a trumpet player\! And at his New Year’s Concert in 2020 he did something no conductor had ever done there before — he picked up his trumpet and played a solo himself\!

**Adults:** Did you know? The Latvian conductor Andris Nelsons, born in Riga in 1978, conducted the New Year’s Concert in 2020 — and made a bit of history: playing the trumpet solo in Lumbye’s Postillon-Galopp, he became the first conductor who was not a violinist to perform a solo at the New Year’s Concert. Nelsons began his career as a trumpeter in the Latvian National Opera orchestra and took private lessons with his colleague, fellow Latvian Mariss Jansons; today, Nelsons is music director of the Boston Symphony Orchestra and *Gewandhauskapellmeister* in Leipzig 

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? In the 2020 concert he conducted, Nelsons also played a trumpet solo, a fun twist on an old Viennese tradition where violinist-conductors such as Boskovsky and Maazel as well as Strauss Jr. himself. Nelsons — a trained trumpeter — became the first non-violinist to play a solo at the concert, in Lumbye’s “Postillon-Galopp”. He owes part of his career path to his countryman Mariss Jansons, having come to Jansons’s attention after substituting in the Oslo Philharmonic’s trumpet section in an emergency during a tour, and counts him as a mentor, having studied privately with him since 2002\. Nelsons has led the Boston Symphony since 2014 and the Leipzig *Gewandhaus* since 2018

# Yannick Nézet-Séguin (2026)

**Kids:** Did you know? Yannick Nézet-Séguin led the New Year’s Concert for the very first time in 2026 — and he was the first conductor from Canada ever to do it\! He even helped make the music for a big Hollywood movie about a famous conductor. 

**Adults:** Did you know? When the Canadian conductor Yannick Nézet-Séguin led the New Year’s Concert on 1 January 2026, it was his first time conducting it — and he was the first Canadian to do so. One of the busiest conductors in the world, he has been music director of the Metropolitan Opera in New York since 2018, of the Philadelphia Orchestra since 2012, and of Montréal’s Orchestre Métropolitain for 25 years. He first appeared with the Vienna Philharmonic in 2010, at the Mozart Week in Salzburg. 

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know?  Nézet-Séguin’s 2026 debut continued the orchestra’s recent turn toward younger conductors. Born in Montréal in 1975 and trained at the Conservatoire de musique du Québec, he holds three of North America’s most prominent posts at once — the Met, Philadelphia and Montréal — and is noted for adventurous programming, including conducting the first work by a Black composer ever staged at the Met, Terence Blanchard’s Fire Shut Up in My Bones. Beyond the concert hall, he served as conducting consultant and conductor for new recordings on the 2023 film Maestro, about Leonard Bernstein. 

# Tugan Sokhiev (2027)

**Kids:** Did you know? The next New Year’s concert conductor was chosen almost a year before the concert\! His name is Tugan Sokhiev, and he comes from a mountain region called North Ossetia. He first conducted when he was just 17 years old, and in 2027 he leads the famous concert series for the very first time. 

**Adults:** Did you know? The conductor announced for the upcoming 2027 concert is Tugan Sokhiev — and when he takes the podium he will be the first Russian conductor to lead it. Born in Vladikavkaz, in North Ossetia, in 1977, he was one of the last pupils of the legendary teacher Ilya Musin at the St Petersburg Conservatory. He has been a close partner of the Vienna Philharmonic since 2009, and in 2025 he made his debut at their Summer Night Concert at Schönbrunn and conducted their gala for the 200th anniversary of Johann Strauss II. 

**Experts / nerds:** Did you know? Sokhiev’s New Year’s Concert was announced by the Vienna Philharmonic in January 2026 as the conductor for the 1 January 2027 concert, which will be his first, and will make him the first Russian conductor in the event’s history. He built his career across France and Russia: music director of the Orchestre national du Capitole de Toulouse (2008–2022) and of Moscow’s Bolshoi Theatre (2014–2022). In March 2022 he resigned from both posts, citing the pressure to condemn the Russian invasion of Ukraine. From the 2026–27 season he has been principal conductor of the Orchestre de la Suisse Romande in Geneva.
